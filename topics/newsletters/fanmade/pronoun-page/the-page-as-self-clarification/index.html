The Page as Self-Clarification

The page did not only explain me to other people. It also cleared things up for me. Having to answer all these questions about myself as I make them did make me clearer to myself.

I think it made me switch to he/it pronouns instead of just saying he/him, though I will still say that second version for brevity or in spaces that are less ubiquitously queer.

I realized that I was touting my virtual girl personality above my agender identity, despite my agender identity being in-person as well as online. Agender, in the way I use it, means that gender categories do not meaningfully describe me. I think it’s easy for me to accidentally do that because my agender identity is very non-confrontational, like it specifically is about the rejection of gender categories for myself. Because it’s a rejection of categories, I often leave it off my labels in the first place. Even just between writing this and having made my pronoun page, I had to go back in and add the agender flag. Not because I forgot, but because it’s so much about not defining myself that it often is the thing not feeling like I need to define.
